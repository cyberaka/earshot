## Decisions taken during the build (proposals, for the owner to confirm)

- **Port 8090** for the service: 8080 is Mint's on this machine. ML 8091, capture 8092, Postgres 5434.
- **Diarization fallback**: Community-1 is gated; until it is downloaded, diarization clusters WeSpeaker
  embeddings of 1.5 s windows over VAD speech. It picks up Community-1 automatically once cached.
- **Chunked transcription**: Whisper runs on groups of VAD speech regions (≤ 28 s), with the previous
  text as prompt. Whole-span transcription of mostly silent channel audio lost punctuation and words.
- **Live capture → ML** is a WebSocket of 16 kHz PCM frames, plus 30 s FLAC chunks on disk.
- **"Replay audio files"** is a capture source, for tests and demos without a meeting.
- **Fonts are bundled** (`@fontsource`), not fetched from Google Fonts, because of NF-1.
- Deletes use an inline two-step button instead of a browser confirm dialog.

## Not built yet / known gaps

- Conversations are segmented per recording file, not across the day's files (PRD §5.2 step 4); a
  conversation split by the recorder's file boundary stays split, and a later import does not
  re-segment an earlier file.
- `ambient_audio_retention_days` is a setting but not enforced (the working copy is per recording).
- No automated socket-blocking test for NF-1 yet; verified manually that every listener is on
  loopback and the UI makes no external requests.
- No Testcontainers integration tests or end-to-end test against a stub LLM yet (PRD §13.2).
- The headphone check reads the default output device name; a Multi-Output Device is resolved with
  `system_profiler`, which is a heuristic.
- Live mode uses the same 7B model as batch (`llm_live_model`); a 3–4B model would cut answer latency.
- Real recorder audio has not been tested: the M-1 spike is still the first thing to do with real data.

## Fix: online call on a 1-channel input (meeting 3, reported by the owner)

Starting **Online call** with "System default" (a 1-channel mic; BlackHole not installed) left a meeting
"live" with capture failing in the background: `Invalid number of channels [PaErrorCode -9998]`.
- Capture now opens the device inside the Start request and refuses a device with too few channels
  (HTTP 400 with a readable reason); the service rolls back the meeting rows, folder and live session.
- The start form checks channels before Start (including the system default), explains that BlackHole
  and an Aggregate Device are needed, and preselects a multi-channel input when one exists.
- A meeting that captured no audio is discarded on Stop instead of queuing a post-meeting pass.
- On service start, meetings still marked live are recovered: capture is stopped and what was recorded
  is processed, or the meeting is discarded if nothing was. Meeting 3 was discarded this way.

## Fix: one voice split into 26 speakers in room audio (meeting 6, owner's test)

Owner played a YouTube video (one narrator) through a Bluetooth speaker, recorded in In-person mode with
the MacBook mic, and spoke the last two sentences. Transcription was fine; the fallback diarizer split
the narrator into 26 clusters. Measured on that recording: with 1.5 s windows the narrator's windows
scored a median 0.33 against each other (0.08 against the owner); 3 s windows raise that to 0.49.
Grid over thresholds on this recording and the synthetic set: 3 s windows with 0.65–0.70 got every
case right. Now 3 s / 1.5 s hop, threshold 0.68, and clusters under 3 s folded into the nearest one.
Result: meeting 6 → narrator + Me; synthetic day unchanged (Me accuracy 100%).
The owner's Me match was 0.62 against a 0.6 threshold: marginal; more Me samples from real audio help.

## Feature: live speaker separation and naming during a meeting (owner request)

- ML `LiveDiarizer` per channel: each utterance's 3 s windows get an instant label from the nearest
  speaker; every 30 s of audio all windows are re-clustered (threshold 0.68) and mapped back onto the
  existing labels by overlap, so labels and names stay stable; changed lines are relabelled in place.
  Simulation vs offline: meeting 7 (real two-person podcast) 98% (a running-centroid-only version: 68%);
  all other recordings 100%.
- Service: each live label is a speaker_cluster (matched live to Me and the library); `speakers` and
  `relabel` events reach the browser; naming a speaker (same API as after a meeting) updates the live
  view at once. Summaries during the meeting use the speakers' names.
- UI: speaker bar above the live transcript (click to name), clickable names on lines.
- Live test (3 min of meeting 7 replayed): 2 speakers, 1 line relabelled by regrouping, a speaker named
  mid-meeting relabelled all its lines, Stop finished in 1.3 s with names kept.

## Feature: resume a meeting later (owner request; shown on every day it has a part)

- V3: `conversation.part`, `conversation.capture_files`, `speaker_cluster.origin_cluster_id`.
- `POST /api/meetings/{id}/resume`: new recording + conversation (part n, "(continued)"); speakers from earlier

## 2026-10-20: Headphone check from the audio, not the output setting

The pre-Start warning ("Sound is playing through the built-in speakers") read the macOS default output. Zoom
picks its own speaker (Call Output Jabra), so it was a false alarm. Replaced by `earshot_ml/echo.py`: the capture
process compares 10 ms loudness envelopes of the mic and system channels every second over 4 s, lags −100 to
+300 ms; correlation ≥ 0.5 in 4 of the last 10 checks with call audio sets `echo.leaking` in the capture status,
and the live meeting shows "Your mic is picking up the call". Mic leakage below RMS 0.004 is ignored.

Measured on the owner's devices (14 s of speech played through each Multi-Output, recorded on its Aggregate):

| Output → Aggregate | Leaking | Score | Mic RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Call Output Mac (speakers) → Aggregate Mac | yes | 0.86 | 0.031 |
| Call Output Jabra (headset) → Aggregate Jabra | no | 0.00 | 0.0003 |

Synthetic: headphones 0.07, leak 0.94. A replayed leaky meeting showed the warning in the live status within
about 8 s of call speech. Gap: when the user talks over the call nonstop, the check misses the leak (turn-taking
is needed to see it), so the warning can come late in such a meeting.

## 2026-10-20: Switch input during a live meeting

Owner request: moving from speakers to headset mid-call must not need Stop/Resume. The live header has an
**Input** dropdown (Aggregate-style devices for an online call, any input otherwise). `POST /api/meetings/{id}/device`
→ capture `POST /capture/{id}/device`: the new device opens before the old one closes, the old device's last
queued blocks (≤ 0.1 s) are dropped, and the channel timeline, transcript and part carry on. A device that can't
work (e.g. a 1-channel mic for an online call) is refused with the reason and recording continues on the old one.
The leak check restarts on the new device.

Verified: live meeting on Aggregate Mac → Jabra (API) → bad device (400) → Mac → Jabra (UI); channel time kept
pace with the clock throughout. Test meetings deleted. Gap: the device list is read at page load and can't be
rescanned while recording (PortAudio rescans close open streams), so a headset plugged in after Start won't appear
until the next meeting; its Aggregate must exist before Start.

## 2026-10-20: Second Zoom call (meeting 15): siblings, line reassignment, en+hi rule rewritten

Call with Aryaman, Rudraansh, Anindita and the owner. Remembered voices worked (Aryaman and Anindita
named live with "Remember"; a later rematch also recognised meeting 13's laptop voice as Anindita, 0.70).

- **Rudraansh merged into Aryaman.** Same phone, siblings: 0.57–0.67 cosine to Aryaman vs 0.81–0.88 for
  Aryaman with himself; the live diarizer only splits below ~0.32 (window embeddings are noisy), so no
  threshold change. Added per-line reassignment instead (V5: `segment.speaker_manual`,
  `speaker_cluster.remember`; `PATCH /api/segments/{id}/speaker`). Moved lines are pinned against live
  relabels, a person's moved lines share one `line:<person>` cluster, and voices are rebuilt from lines
  (`PeopleService.refreshVoice`, ≤120 s of the longest lines) in the background; during a live meeting
  from the channel's 30 s chunk directory (`audio.read_span` on a directory; `/ml/embed` answers 409 when
  the audio isn't written yet), and for every touched remembered voice at Stop.
  Replay test: moving one Rudraansh line to a test person, then Stop → rematch, relabelled his other
  38 s cluster to that person by voice while Aryaman's stayed Aryaman. Test meeting/person deleted.
- **Language ran as `en`:** most likely a tab loaded before the en+hi default. The live header now shows
  the language and can switch it (`POST /api/meetings/{id}/language` → `/ml/live/{session}/language`).
- **en+hi rule was wrong.** Experiment over all 115 utterances ≥1.5 s of meetings 13 and 15 (en, hi,
  and Whisper's language probability per line):
  - Hindi forced onto English loops ("इसी कि इसी कि …") or drifts into Cyrillic/Hangul/Hebrew, and loops
    score *better* than the real English (-0.2..-0.4): the old rule picked garbage in 5 of its 7 Hindi picks.
  - Hinglish ("सिर्फ़ English words को transcribe") scored -0.41 as an English translation: never retried.
  - Whisper's language detection rated that Hinglish line 97% English: useless as a pre-check.
  - New rule: retry unless English ≥ -0.25; a text-only Hindi decode from one encoder run (0.07 s
    decode, ~0.66 s with the encoder vs 1.57 s for a full pass with word timings, which encodes twice);
    keep Hindi only if Devanagari, not garbled (repeats, other scripts, compression > 2), ≥ -0.5 on its
    own and > English + 0.05; then the full Hindi pass. Result: 3 of 115 lines changed, all correct,
    no garbage; children's muddy phone audio stays English. Also recovers meeting 13's opening Hindi
    that `en` reduced to "Thank you.". Cost: RTF 0.46 → 0.64 on these lines (+40%).
